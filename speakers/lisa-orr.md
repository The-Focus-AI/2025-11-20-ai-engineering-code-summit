## Speaker Session: "Your Support Team Should Ship Code"

### Key Thesis

Lisa presented a compelling case study demonstrating how support teams—traditional non-engineering roles—can be empowered to ship production code by leveraging AI coding tools. The session challenged conventional organizational structures that isolate support from engineering.

### Core Insights

**The Support-to-Engineering Pipeline**
- Support team members participating in Zapier's AI-assisted coding experiment have become so proficient that they're transitioning directly into engineering roles
- These are customer support specialists who possess intimate knowledge of user pain points, now equipped to ship fixes directly
- This represents a fundamental shift in how organizations can leverage existing talent

**Scout MCP + Cursor: The Technology Stack**
Zapier's approach combines two key tools:
- **Scout MCP** - Built on Cursor and MCP (Model Context Protocol) standards
- **Cursor** - The AI-powered code editor driving the agent's capabilities
- Together, these tools enable support teams to diagnose issues, generate fixes, and validate solutions

**Measured Results**
- **70% Accuracy Rate** - Scout achieves approximately 70% accuracy on fixes and fixability assessments
- **2x Velocity Increase** - Support team velocity doubled during the pilot
- **3-4 Fixes Per Week** - With Scout assistance, support team members are shipping 3-4 fixes weekly
- **40% of Videos Done by Scout** - The tool is handling 40% of support team troubleshooting videos

### The Support + Code Generation Formula

Lisa identified the synergistic power of combining support expertise with code generation:

**Why Support Teams Are Uniquely Positioned**
- **Closest to Customer Pain** - Support teams interface directly with users and understand real-world problems
- **Real-Time Troubleshooting** - They work in the moment with customer issues, enabling rapid problem diagnosis
- **Best at Validation** - Support teams intuitively understand whether a fix actually solves the user's problem

**The Workflow**
1. **Diagnose** - Identify the root cause of issues
2. **Plan, Execute, Validate** - Orchestrate the fix, implement it, and verify the solution works
3. **Backlog Contribution** - Even when issues aren't immediately fixable, the analysis identifies patterns worth prioritizing

### The Grand Canyon Metaphor

Lisa opened with a thoughtful parallel to the Grand Canyon's formation through erosion—drawing an analogy to how support issues accumulate and erode product quality. AI agents, properly orchestrated and tiered together, can address these patterns of degradation.

### Technical Architecture

**The Agent Framework**
- Leverages Scout MCP and Cursor MCP protocols for standardized agent communication
- Enables orchestration—combining multiple tools and agents to solve complex problems
- The true value emerges from tiering these tools together, not using them in isolation

**Dogfooding at Scale**
Zapier's approach is deeply rooted in eating its own dog food:
- Uses Zapier's automation capabilities to orchestrate the support-to-code workflow
- Tests AI agents on real customer problems, not synthetic scenarios
- Validates solutions with actual customers

### Broader Organizational Impact

**Role Transformation**
This initiative demonstrates a powerful organizational capability: converting customer-facing expertise into product development capacity. Rather than requiring support staff to gain computer science degrees, Zapier is enabling talented problem-solvers to contribute code directly.

**The Data Backing the Vision**
According to the conference's coverage of Zapier's work:
- This is "what might be the conference's most striking datapoint"
- Demonstrates that support teams "have achieved such proficiency that they're moving into the engineering team"
- Represents a fundamental shift in how organizations can structure themselves around AI capabilities

## The Organizational Implication

Lisa's session addressed one of the summit's core questions: **Can AI actually transform how work gets organized?**

Her answer, grounded in real Zapier data: **Yes, when you empower people closest to the problem.**

Rather than asking "How do we automate support?", Zapier asked "How do we enable support teams to ship code?" The distinction matters. It's not about replacement—it's about role elevation. Support specialists who spend years understanding customer problems gain the tools to implement solutions. This creates a natural pipeline from support into engineering, bypassing traditional academic gatekeeping.

## Research Notes

**Key Statistics from Lisa's Presentation**
- 70% accuracy on issue diagnosis and fix generation
- 2x velocity improvement (specific metric: 3-4 fixes/week with Scout)
- 40% of support troubleshooting videos now handled by Scout

**The Scout MCP Project**
Scout represents Zapier's internal MCP implementation purpose-built for support automation. Built on Cursor's capabilities and following MCP standards, it enables:
- Automated diagnosis of customer issues
- Code generation for fixes
- Validation against real customer scenarios
- Integration with Zapier's automation platform

**Why This Matters**
Lisa's work at Zapier provides empirical evidence for a controversial thesis: that AI's impact on employment might not be simple substitution, but rather role transformation. Support teams aren't being replaced—they're being upgraded, with career paths that were previously blocked now opening through technical enablement.

The economic implication is profound: organizations get both (1) dramatically increased support team velocity AND (2) an internal pipeline of engineers who understand actual customer problems intimately.

## Impact on Software Development Practice

**Support as Engineering's Early Warning System**
When support teams can ship code, they become:
- Real-time quality assurance (problems surface immediately)
- Product feedback integrated into the code pipeline
- Emergency response capacity (critical issues get fixed in hours, not sprints)

**The Missing Link in Traditional Orgs**
Most enterprises separate support (reactive, cost center) from engineering (proactive, value center). Zapier's model bridges this gap, making support simultaneously reactive (addressing immediate customer pain) and proactive (preventing similar issues).
