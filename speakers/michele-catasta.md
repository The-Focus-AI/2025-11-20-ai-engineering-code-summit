## About Replit
Replit is an AI-powered cloud development platform democratizing software creation. With 22+ million creators and 500,000+ business users, Replit enables anyone to build professional web applications using natural language. The platform evolved from a collaborative coding IDE to an agentic software creation ecosystem, with Replit Agent advancing to fully autonomous capabilities that can test, fix, and deploy applications autonomously for extended periods (200+ minutes).

## Speaker Background
Michele Catasta is a leading AI researcher and executive with deep expertise in Large Language Models, agents, and AI for code. He earned a Ph.D. in Computer Science from EPFL and previously served as Research Scientist and Instructor at Stanford University's Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (SAIL), where he pioneered Transformer applications to source code starting in 2018. Before joining Replit, he was Head of Applied Research at Google Labs and Google X, contributing to PaLM and PaLM 2's coding capabilities. He co-founded Sindice.com (the largest Semantic Web Search Engine), which evolved into Siren, an investigative intelligence platform that secured $15M+ in funding. At Replit, Michele architected and led the launch of Replit Agent v1 and v2, driving over two orders of magnitude in company revenue growth.

## Research Notes
Michele's key innovation focus at Replit centers on the paradigm shift from AI copilots to fully autonomous agents. His session "Autonomy Is All You Need" addresses how AI must move beyond support roles to true autonomous task completion. Key architectural insights from his work include: managing context through persistent filesystem storage and code base understanding; implementing sub-agents to avoid "context pollution" by maintaining fresh context for specialized tasks; and orchestrating parallel agents through a core loop that decides parallelism on-the-fly. He emphasizes practical agent design principles: limiting agent scope to specific, well-defined tasks; maintaining human control over aspects users care about; and maximizing "irreducible runtime" through tasks following natural complexity progression (plan → implement → test → loop). His focus on autonomous testing addresses a critical bottleneck in agent-driven development, breaking the feedback loop that accumulates small errors. Michele is also an active angel investor with 100+ investments in the AI space and serves as advisor to Bessemer, Coatue, Fellows Fund, and Obvious. He began his transformer research at Stanford in 2018 and has published extensively in information retrieval, machine learning, and deep learning, earning 16,000+ citations.
